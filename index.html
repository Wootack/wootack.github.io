About

My research interests include mobile computing, augmented reality, and on-device DNN execution. Through my research, I reveal new workload patterns from new applications and propose application-specific system techniques to solve the issues they raise.

I build perception systems for environments where compute is limited, power is finite, and reliability matters. My work spans the stack from hardware to intelligent systems.

At the hardware level, I study how neural networks can remain robust when SRAM is pushed below safe operating voltages and begins to produce bit-flips. At the sensing level, I explore how far LiDAR data can be compressed while preserving downstream detection accuracy. At the system level, MERCI asks a broader question: when no single model can efficiently cover an entire visual vocabulary, how can inputs be routed adaptively to specialized experts without incurring the full cost of an ensemble?

The common theme across these projects is constraint. Real-world systems operate under budgets for energy, latency, memory, and supervision. I am interested in the design space that emerges precisely when those limits become unavoidable.

I am a combined M.S./Ph.D. student at the Human-Centered Computer Systems Lab at Seoul National University.
